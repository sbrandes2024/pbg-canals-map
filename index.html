    Main drainage routes

    1. Neighborhood lakes. Many lakes and ponds in Palm Beach Gardens neighborhoods are stormwater lakes. They are linked by underground pipes, culverts and small canals, many run by the Northern Palm Beach County Improvement District (NPBCID, shaded purple, toggle it on). Those underground pipes are not on this map, so it cannot tell you which outlet a particular lake uses.

    2. C-17 (Earman River Canal) and S-44. The official SFWMD GIS shows the C-17 canal draining east through the S-44 control structure to the Lake Worth Lagoon, which is part of the Intracoastal Waterway near North Palm Beach. From there water reaches the ocean through the Lake Worth (Palm Beach) Inlet.

    3. C-18 (Hungryland Slough) and S-46. The official SFWMD GIS shows the C-18 canal draining through the S-46 structure into the Southwest Fork of the Loxahatchee River, which reaches the ocean at Jupiter Inlet.

    4. Other local outlets. Some areas, especially near the coast, may drain to the Intracoastal through local outfalls or creeks such as Lake Worth Creek. Not all of these are shown.

    Known gaps

    • Underground pipes and culverts that connect lakes and canals are missing.
    • The county water body data is several years old. The newest record change in this area is from June 2022.
    • The route labels on the map (for example "C-17 Earman River → Intracoastal") were placed by hand as a guide. They are not official GIS features.

    Which route a given lake uses depends on its local district permit and pipes. Confirm with NPBCID (npbcid.org) or the city before relying on it.

    Disclaimer. This map uses the most recent publicly available data we could find from the listed sources. Some records may be outdated, and waterways, structures, and drainage connections may have changed since they were mapped. Connections may be incomplete or unverified. For general reference only, not for navigation, flood risk assessment, or engineering decisions.

    Path to the ocean routes are estimates. They follow official USGS flowlines where they exist, and any hop from a lake to a canal is assumed because underground pipes are not mapped.
